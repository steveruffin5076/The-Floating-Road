# 03 — Story, World & Content

## 1. The world in one page

**Japan, 1648–1651 (Keian era).** Fifty years of peace have curdled something. After
Sekigahara (1600) and the fall of Osaka castle (1615), the shogunate confiscated domain
after domain — each confiscation threw hundreds of samurai onto the roads with swords
and no stipends. The Kan'ei famine (1640–43) emptied villages into the same roads.
The bakuto run gambling dens and loan-sharking in every town's shadow. And in Edo, a
military scholar named **Yui Shōsetsu** has been quietly teaching strategy to exactly
the men the peace has discarded — and whispering that when the old shōgun Iemitsu
dies, the child who replaces him will not survive the autumn.

The player is never the shōgun and never a daimyō. You are the people *underneath*
history: a masterless samurai, a runaway farmer, a debtor, a gambler, a woman who
should not be traveling alone. The Keian Uprising is not your story — it is the
weather your story happens in. Every run ends in autumn 1651, when the plot unravels:
Marubashi Chūya betrays it in a fever, is crucified in Edo on September 24th; Yui
Shōsetsu commits seppuku at Sunpu on September 10th; the conspirators' families are
executed with them. Where your character stands when that news arrives is the final
question every Tale asks.

**Rules of the world (strictly historical):**
- No magic, no yokai, no anachronisms. If something feels supernatural, it is rumor,
  and the codex entry explains the real history behind the rumor.
- Class is physics. A peasant who strikes a samurai dies. A samurai who works with
  his hands loses face. A merchant with money can bend almost any rule but never
  break the sword-law.
- Violence is fast, ugly, and legally radioactive. Every killing raises Suspicion
  unless witnessed as lawful (duel with seconds, execution, defense with witnesses).
- Hunger, weather, and debt kill more people than swords. The game agrees.

## 2. Geography & locations

```
 KYOTO ──(Sanjō bridge)── OTSU ── KUSATSU ── KANEYA ── … 53 stations …
   ── ODAWARA ── HAKONE [SEKISHO — strict] ── NUMAZU ──
   ── SUNPU (Keian secondary target) ── OI RIVER [no bridge, no ferry] ──
   ── KAKEGAWA ── HAMAMATSU ── … ── HAKONE/ARAI passes ──
   ── KANAGAWA ── KAWASAKI ── SHINAGAWA ── EDO (Nihonbashi, zero-mile)
```

**Event location pools:** `road` (pine highway, ichirizuka mounds), `village`,
`post-town` (hatago inns, teahouses, toiyaba porter offices), `river-ford` (Ōi, Abe,
Tenryū), `mountain-pass` (Hakone), `checkpoint`, `night`, `city-edo` (Nihonbashi
market, Kanda, Yoshiwara edge, Kodenmachō prison district, temple grounds),
`city-osaka` (rice warehouses, docks), `city-kyoto` (court-adjacent, artisan wards).

Default direction of travel: **west → east** (Kyoto toward Edo) for Tales 2, 3, 5;
Tale 1 and 4 can start either direction (rōnin fleeing Osaka's shadow / gambler
circuit follows the den network). Direction is a run-setting at creation.

## 3. The shared spine (all Tales)

Three "omen" events fire for every character regardless of Tale, escalating per act —
this is the connective tissue LiA lacked:

1. **Act 1 — "Men With No Banners":** you meet rōnin begging, or a procession of
   confiscated-domain samurai walking east with their household goods. A conversation
   plants the phrase *"the world is upside down, and someone will have to right it."*
2. **Act 2 — "The Scholar's School":** in Edo's Kanda district (or by letter/rumor
   elsewhere), the name Yui Shōsetsu surfaces: a strategy teacher who takes rōnin
   students and pays them nothing but hope. Characters with Chi ≥ 6 can read between
   his lines.
3. **Act 3 — "Fever Talk":** September 1651. Depending on the character's position,
   the uprising's collapse reaches them as: participation, arrest-sweep danger,
   rumor at a teahouse, or a head on display at Kodenmachō. Every ending epilogue
   references where you stood.

### TALE 1 — THE MASTERLESS BLADE (rōnin) *(default)*

**Premise.** Your clan served a Tozama daimyō dispossessed after Osaka. Your father
died in the siege; your karo (chief retainer), **Katsuragi Hyōgo**, walked out of the
castle with the surrender papers — and a Tokugawa stipend. You kept two things: a
katana with your family's crest-ground blade, and a name on a list of "unattached
swordsmen" the sekisho are told to watch for. It is spring 1648. You are walking east.

**Cast.**
- **Katsuragi Hyōgo** — the collaborator. Now a minor Edo official's hatamoto-adjacent
  functionary. Neither cartoon villain nor saint: he sold the clan to save 400
  households from execution, and believes it. Confronting him is the Tale's crux.
- **O-Ryō** — a widow running a teahouse at Mariko station; ex-Toyotomi camp follower;
  information broker for drifting samurai. (Recurring across Tales.)
- **Yui Shōsetsu** — historical. Calm, courteous, terrifyingly reasonable. Offers the
  player's character "a cause worth a sword."
- **Marubashi Chūya** — historical. Spearman, ex-Owari retainer, coughs blood,
  talks grandeur. If befriended, his fever-betrayal in Act 3 devastates the player.
- **Sagawa Jin'emon** — fictional dōshin (Edo police official) who appears across
  Tales whenever Suspicion is high; not evil, just thorough.

**Act beats.**
- Act 1: road events + chain: *[Watch-list]* (checkpoint recognizes your sword crest —
  bluff/bribe/fight), *[The Duel at Fujieda]* (a kabukimono picks a fight with a
  peasant; intervene = Reputation+, Suspicion+ / walk on = Resolve−), *[Old Retainer]*
  (find dying clan-servant who gives a letter proving Katsuragi's bargain).
- Act 2 (Edo): choose a livelihood — bodyguard work, sword-school assistant, sumo
  exhibition, or the underworld; chain: *[The Scholar's Invitation]* (meet Yui;
  recruit / refuse / inform), *[Katsuragi's Garden]* (locate him; three approaches:
  petition for legal **adauchi** authorization (Chi/Kuchi marathon, slow, lawful),
  challenge to illegal hatashiai duel (Waza, fast, criminal), or blackmail (Me/Kuchi,
  Aku slide), *[The Fever Friend]* (Marubashi chain if you joined Yui).
- Act 3 (Keian): if recruited — you are in Sunpu or Edo when the plot collapses:
  fight your way out, stand and die, seppuku, or flee the country roads forever. If
  not recruited — the arrest sweeps come anyway for anyone with Suspicion ≥ 3; or you
  witness the aftermath and finally face Katsuragi with the city on edge.

**Endings (12).**
| Ending | Route | Key requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **A New Banner** (bright) | Legal revenge path abandoned; earn adoption/employment under an honest lord via bodyguard arc | Reputation ≥ +3, Suspicion ≤ 1, completed 2+ employer contracts, refused Yui |
| **The Lawful Vendetta** (solemn) | Adauchi authorized, Katsuragi killed in sanctioned duel, you submit to review | Chi ≥ 8 or Kuchi ≥ 8, item: father's letter, Suspicion ≤ 2, Reputation ≥ +1 |
| **First Blood at Dawn** (dark-neutral) | Illegal hatashiai; you win, then live as hunted | Waza ≥ 9, won duel, escaped Sagawa chain |
| **The Garden Gate** (dark) | Blackmail → Katsuragi's hired thugs → mutual destruction | Aku ≥ +3 |
| **Sunpu, September 10th** (tragic-heroic) | Joined Yui; at Sunpu, seppuku as the walls close — historically accurate | Joined plot, reached Sunpu in Act 3, Resolve ≥ 6 |
| **The Fever Betrayal** (tragic) | Joined; tried to warn Marubashi; died in the Edo arrest sweep | Joined, befriended Marubashi, in Edo Act 3 |
| **Informant** (grim-survival) | Sold Yui's plan to the metsuke; rewarded, then despised; your name survives in no song | Kuchi ≥ 7, Chi ≥ 6, Aku path |
| **Sword for Sale** (neutral) | Ignore everything; become a famous mercenary instructor — rich, hollow | 4+ combat wins, Reputation ≥ +2, refused all causes |
| **The Plow** (quiet-bright) | Bury the sword; marry into O-Ryō's teahouse or a farm | Resolve ≥ 8, Gi ≥ +2, completed O-Ryō chain |

### TALE 2 — THE FAMINE ROAD (runaway peasant)

**Premise.** Kan'ei famine took your village in Mikawa: first the seed rice, then the
bracken roots, then your little sister. The village headman's granary had rice — tax
rice, sealed for the daimyō. The night your neighbor's family hanged themselves, five
households (the gonin-gumi) made a choice you can never unmake: they broke the seal.
Now the magistrate's men are coming to find who did it, and **a farmer who leaves his
land is already a criminal.** You are walking east with 200 mon sewn into your straw
coat and your mother's wooden prayer tablet.

**Cast.**
- **Jihei** — the headman's son who walked with you; guilt-eaten, talkative, liable to
  get you both killed (companion event chain; can be sent home, lost, or kept).
- **Tōzō** — an old ex-ashigaru turned porter-guild boss at the Ōi river; hires
  runaways without questions; knows exactly what you are.
- **Oyabun Kichizo** — bandit-chief of a hill gang recruiting famine men; the "easy
  road" temptation throughout.
- **Priest Enkai** — a traveling Jizō-cult monk who shelters runaways; gives the Tale
  its conscience.

**Act beats.**
- Act 1: survival tutorial dressed as desperation — food events hit harder (you start
  with almost nothing), work events (porter, night soil, field labor), chain:
  *[The Granary Night]* (flashback, sets Gi/Aku baseline by what you personally did),
  *[Five Households]* (the gonin-gumi pact: magistrates pressure your neighbors'
  letters that catch up with you at stations).
- Act 2 (Edo): the city swallows villages whole. Chain: *[No Papers, No Work]*
  (employment requires temple registration you don't have → forge, or work black:
  construction on the new estates, firefighting crews — the town brigades hire
  strong desperate men), *[Tōzō's Guild]* vs *[Kichizo's Hill]* fork deepens,
  *[A Letter From Mikawa]* — your village is being punished for the granary crime;
  go home and confess (bright-sad), or earn enough to buy the village's fine from
  afar (hard-economic), or let it burn (Aku).
- Act 3: famine relief riots flare in the cities as Keian paranoia tightens policing;
  the rōnin plot's collapse means mass arrests of "suspicious drifters" — you are
  structurally one. Final movement: home, or a new name.

**Endings (10).** Includes: **The Confession** (return, take the beating/executed in
your neighbors' place — village freed; bittersweet-heroic), **Headman Tōzō** (rise
through the porter guild to legitimacy), **Kichizo's Heir** (bandit lord — dark),
**Fireman of Edo** (hero of a blaze, wins papers via magistrate's favor — bright),
**A Name Bought** (pay the village fine anonymously, live in Edo as a laborer —
quiet-bright), **The Tablet** (die on the road home — tragic), plus shared death/
despair family. Requirement examples: *Fireman* needs Chikara ≥ 7 + Tan ≥ 6 +
the fire set-piece + Gi ≥ 0; *Confession* needs the Mikawa letter chain resolved
toward home + Resolve ≥ 5.

### TALE 3 — THE DEBTOR'S LEDGER (townsman)

**Premise.** Your family's Osaka rice-brokerage went under when a shipment sank in a
storm — and the debt, with interest compounding at underworld rates, rolled onto you.
The lender is **Bunzō**, a bakuto oyabun with a teahouse front in Dōtonbori, who
offers a "kindness": 100 ryō, 25% interest per season, or work it off in ways that
stop being errands and start being crimes. Your older brother signed you as guarantor
and has vanished east. You are following his trail to Edo — and the ledger travels
with you, ticking every season.

**Cast.**
- **Bunzō** — charming, patient, unkillable-without-consequence lender. Every Tale 3
  run is a negotiation with him even from 500 km away (his agents appear).
- **Kikunosuke** — your vanished brother; his trail rewrites itself depending on how
  much the player learns (he didn't flee the debt; he fled *to* something — Act 2
  reveal: he's mixed into Yui Shōsetsu's circle, believing the coup will erase all
  debts — "when the shōgun falls, ledgers burn"). Historically-flavored delusion.
- **O-Sumi** — a pawnshop owner's daughter with her own quiet ledger of family
  obligation; ally/romance option (period-plausible courtship via her father's shop).
- **Sagawa Jin'emon** — reappears if your debt-work raised Suspicion.

**Act beats.**
- Act 1: earn-and-spend pressure loop unique to this Tale (season interest events),
  peddling goods between stations is unlocked as a mechanic-teaching chain,
  *[Brother's Sandals]* trail events.
- Act 2 (Edo): *[The Ledger in Kanda]* — find Kikunosuke inside the ronin-plot
  periphery; fork: pay Bunzō's Edo agent clean (needs big money), renegotiate into
  servitude (Aku, safe), or turn evidence of Bunzō's illegal lending to the machi-bugyō
  (dangerous — the bakuto and the police understand each other).
- Act 3: interest finalizes; Keian sweeps make Bunzō nervous — a nervous oyabun calls
  in debts violently. Climax: the confrontation at his teahouse on the night the
  Marubashi arrests begin.

**Endings (10).** Includes: **Ledger Closed** (pay in full — bright, requires
accumulated wealth ≥ 130 ryō-equiv), **The New Bunzō** (take over his territory —
dark, Aku ≥ +4), **Bugyō's Witness** (testify; enter witness protection-ish exile
with a new town registration — neutral-bright, Chi/Kuchi gates), **Brothers in
Kodenmachō** (Kikunosuke arrested as conspirator, you take his debt-cell — tragic),
**Dōtonbori Water** (default-failure death), **The Pawnshop** (marry into O-Sumi's
family business, debt absorbed as bride-price negotiation — quiet-bright, needs her
chain + Gi ≥ +1 + ≥ 40 ryō goodwill).

### TALE 4 — ROLLING BONES (bakuto gambler) *(unlocked after finishing Tales 1–3)*

**Premise.** You grew up holding the cup in your mother's roadside den. You can call
chō-han honest or crooked, and you have a burn scar proving which teacher you had.
When the oyabun of your den is knifed by a rival outfit allied with the **tekiya**
peddler network, the cup passes to nobody — so you walk the Tōkaidō den circuit with
your dice, your scar, and the question of whether you're collecting allies or
collecting a funeral list.

**Mechanics hook:** this Tale *starts with* the chō-han system fully unlocked
(other Tales discover it mid-run) and gets a den-network reputation track instead of
the usual employer track. Gambling is income AND combat-adjacent (den brawls).

**Cast.** **Oyabun "Iron Cup" Gonbei** (your dead mentor, in flashbacks), **Koharu**
(a tekiya peddler boss's sharp-tongued daughter — the rival network is her family;
romance/diplomacy route), **One-Eyed Denpachi** (the knifer; Act 3 antagonist),
plus the historical ninkyō culture of bakuto codes — loyalty oaths, sake-sharing
rituals, finger-forefeiture (depicted once, non-graphic, as a choice with permanent
Waza cost and huge den-network reputation gain — the game's most infamous decision).

**Act beats.** Den circuit events per station (each post-town has a den with its own
tiny state), alliance-building vs solo-vengeance fork, *[The Cup Remembers]*
(flashback chains that teach chō-han mastery upgrades), Act 3: the great den at
Shinagawa on Keian-eve — the shogunate's arrest sweeps hit underworld dens hardest
(they assume ronin plots hide there — historically the sweeps did sweep the margins),
so the finale is a heist-flavored escape/stand during the panic.

**Endings (9).** Includes: **The Cup Passes** (become circuit oyabun — needs 5+ dens
allied), **Ninkyō** (disband your den into honest festival work with Koharu's
tekiya — bright, cross-network marriage alliance), **Denpachi's Grave** (revenge
complete, arrested, executed — dark-tragic), **One-Eyed Pilgrim** (spare Denpachi,
both of you walk away from the life — quiet), **Sweep** (die in the Shinagawa
raids), plus shared family. The finger-forfeit choice gates a secret variant of
**The Cup Passes** ("the oyabun who paid in flesh").

### TALE 5 — THE PILGRIM'S WAY (woman traveling alone) *(omoide unlock)*

**Premise.** Period-accurate difficulty Tale. You are **O-Tsu**, a widow (or a
divorced daughter, chosen at creation) of a minor samurai household dissolved in a
domain confiscation. The law and the road both treat a traveling woman as either
cargo or prey: sekisho inspect women's permits against written descriptions to stop
daimyō families smuggling hostages ("irappo donna"), inns segregate, and every
labor job is closed. Your goal is Ise — then, when Ise doesn't fix anything, whatever
you decide the road is *for*.

**Mechanics hook:** checkpoint events have harsher DCs for you, BUT a whole parallel
economy is open to you that male Tales barely see: temple lodging networks, shrine
maiden work, midwifery (Chi-gated, respected, well-paid), entertainers' guilds,
women's pilgrimage groups (safety in numbers — join a group = fewer night events but
shared money), and the paper-forging trade's premium market.

**Cast.** **O-Kinu** — an Ise-road tea house mistress who runs an informal shelter
network for women in bad marriages (historical: temple "divorce temples" like Tōkei-ji
did exactly this — the Tale can route the player there as a landmark ending),
**Bikuni nun Myōshin** — a wandering fundraiser nun with a forgery sideline,
**Gombei the porter** — a decent man whose decency the game tests repeatedly.

**Act beats.** Act 1 = the permit gauntlet (buy/forge/charm papers; get robbed; join
a pilgrimage group); Act 2 = Ise, then a choice: settle into shrine economy, go east
to Edo to find the household that confiscated your family (revenge-adjacent, but
legal: petition the bugyō as a wronged gentlewoman — Chi/Kuchi marathon), or the
divorce-temple route helping other women; Act 3 = Keian sweeps make independent
travel lethal (unpermitted women are arrested as suspicious by default) — the finale
is getting yourself, and possibly a shelter network of others, *registered* or *out*.

**Endings (8).** Includes: **Tōkei-ji's Gate** (enter the convent's protection
officially — historical, bittersweet-bright), **The Petition** (win your family's
name restored — hard lawful marathon, bright), **Mother of the Road** (run O-Kinu's
network as your own — neutral-bright, needs 3+ sheltered-woman chain events),
**Ise Forever** (shrine life — quiet), plus tragic/death family tuned to the road's
real dangers.

### TALE 6 — THE HIDDEN CROSS (kakure kirishitan) *(post-MVP, omoide unlock)*

**Premise.** Ten years after Shimabara, the shogunate's terror of Christianity is
bureaucratic, patient, and total: temple-registration checks, fumi-e (image-trampling)
tests at checkpoints and during village sweeps, and informants paid per head. You are
from an Urakami-style hidden community near Nagasaki; a relative was taken in a sweep,
and you are carrying something — a rosary, a copied prayer, a name-list — east to the
only people who might help: Portuguese-ship rumors in Edo that everyone knows are
lies. This is the hardest Tale: the contraband in your inventory is a permanent
Suspicion bomb, and the "victory" ending is not victory but survival with your
community intact and unbetrayed. Written with documentary care; the persecution is
never gamified as "fun evil" — the codex treats it as history.

**Endings (6, sketched):** community preserved in hiding (bright-somber), martyrdom
choice at the fumi-e (tragic — handled with total restraint), the name-list burned
(neutral), betrayal-for-survival (darkest choice in the game, Aku −5), plus shared
family. Full design deferred to V1.x pass.

## 5. Historical cameos policy

Real named figures appear **only** where records support their presence and **only**
as the story permits: Yui Shōsetsu and Marubashi Chūya (Tale 1/3, 1648–51 Edo —
documented), Sagawa the dōshin is fictional. Miyamoto Musashi (died 1645) appears
**never in person** — only his book, his students, and his legend, which keeps us
honest. O-Tsu (Tale 5 name) is deliberately a common period name, not the *Musashi
novel* character; we will rename if it reads as reference.

## 7. Sample events (fully written, showing target voice & schema)

> Format: **ID / pool / act / requirements — body — choices [check] → outcomes.**
> Tone guide: present tense, second person, ≤300 words per node, concrete sensory
> detail, no modern idiom, Japanese terms glossed once in-text.

### Sample 1 — `checkpoint_hakone_papers` (checkpoint, Act 1–2, all Tales)

> The barrier at Hakone sits across the road like a closed hand. Pine timbers, a
> tiled guardhouse, and the notice board where the shogunate's fears are posted in
> ink: *women entering Edo. Guns leaving Edo.* Three guards warm their hands over a
> brazier. The line ahead of you is eleven people long, and the inspector is
> reading each permit aloud, slowly, comparing face to paper.
>
> You have papers. The question is what kind.

- **Present your permit honestly.** [none — outcome branches on permit quality item & Tale]
  - Good papers: The inspector grunts. The gate-arm lifts. (Advance, no cost.)
  - No/weak papers (Tale 2, 5, 6 default): "Temple certificate?" You have none that matches your face. He sets your paper down and looks at you for a long moment. → sub-branch below.
- **Talk your way through — the dialect of a man who belongs here.** [Kuchi DC 5]
  - Success: You answer his questions before he finishes asking, mention your uncle's shop in Odawara, and complain about the rain the way locals do. He waves you through, already reading the next paper. (Advance. If Tale 2: flag `guard_face_remembered` 20%.)
  - Failure: "Odawara, is it. My brother-in-law lives in Odawara." His voice doesn't change. "Name him." You can't. Two guards step off the brazier. → *Arrested* (Suspicion +2, money −50%, branch to `arrest_chain`) or *Run* [Chikara DC 6 — success: into the pines, −Resolve 2, flagged; failure: captured].

### Sample 2 — `night_tsujigiri` (night/road, Act 1–2, all Tales)

> Rain stopped an hour ago. The road steams. Somewhere behind you, sandal-slaps on
> wet stone — too fast, then stopping when you slow. In 1602 the shogunate banned

- **Turn and face him now, on your ground.** [Me DC 3 to see his stance; then combat at your choice of stance]
  - He is young. His sleeves are tied back with a woman's cord. Combat (foe Power 8, no surprise). Win → loot: *unmarked blade, condition 100%* + **the decision**: it is evidence and a good sword both. (Keep: Suspicion-risk flag `cursed_blade_carried` if any witness events fire; Leave it for the magistrates: Reputation +1, Gi +1.)

  - Failure: Bamboo rhizomes take your ankle. → *Cornered* (combat at enemy +15%)
- **Shout for the next village's night-watch. Fire-bell rope, fifty paces ahead.** [requires location knowledge flag from prior road event, OR Chi ≥ 5]

### Sample 3 — `posttown_chohan_den` (post-town, Act 1–3, all Tales; Tale 4 gets extended version)

> The den is a hut of bamboo and indigo cloth behind the horse-market, lantern-lit
> from inside like a paper fish. A doorman with a burn scar checks your hands, then
> your face. Inside: a straw mat, eight men, a lacquer cup, and two dice. The
> dealer's call runs like a temple chant — *"Hanko, hanko, chō-han na…"* He flips
> the cup. **"Chō!"** Even. Half the mat groans; the other half rakes in copper.
> Minimum bet: 20 mon. The house takes a tenth.

- **Play honest. One round.** (chō-han mini-game: call chō/han; 50/50 minus house
  cut; can stop anytime — greed is the mechanic)
- **Play deep.** (same, stakes ×5; at loss ≥ 100 mon the dealer offers a loan —
  Tale 3 special dialogue: *"Bunzō-san sends his regards."*)
- **Watch the cup. Not the dice — the dealer's wrist.** [Me DC 6]
  - Success: He favors chō on the third shake — a wrist-lock tells. You win four
    rounds in a row. The mat goes quiet in a way that has edges. (money +160 mon,
    flag `den_marked_you` → next den visit at this town: hostile variant)
  - Failure: Your eyes moved. His stopped. "Friend," the dealer says, warmly, "you
    have a *stare* problem." Two men stand up. → *Leave winnings and go* (−all
    round's profit) or *Accuse him of loaded dice first* [Kuchi DC 7 — brilliant
    desperate gambit: success → he cuts you in for 10% of the night (Tale 4 hook);
    failure → combat vs `den_enforcers` Power 10, in a hut, with witnesses]
- **Cheat: sleeve the die you palmed from the mat.** [Waza DC 7]
  - Success: +250 mon. −Gi 1. Flag `cheated_den` (Tale 4: the cup remembers —
    Koharu's people hear about it; reputation among gamblers becomes double-edged).
  - Failure: Burn scars, they say, come from holding hot dice. They don't tell you
    whose hand was holding. → *Caught* (fingers broken: −Health 4, permanent −1
    Waza for the run, thrown out) or *Buy your hand back* (all money).
- *(Tale 4 only)* **Show your scar. Ask whose circuit this is.** → chain event.

### Sample 4 — `road_oi_river_ford` (river-ford, Act 1–2, all Tales)

> The Ōi has no bridge and no ferry, because the shogunate wants it that way: a

### Sample 5 — `posttown_drunk_hatamoto` (post-town/night, Act 1–3; the kirisute-gomen pressure event)

## 8. Ending compendium & collection meta

- ~50 endings at full content (12+10+10+9+8+6 across Tales ≈ 55 minus overlap with
  shared death family).
- Ending screen: epilogue (150–250 words, past tense, "years later" framing), earned
  **title** (e.g. *The Patient One*, *Cup-Bearer of Shinagawa*), omoide payout,
  **historical note** card (real facts: e.g. for *Sunpu, September 10th* — the
  actual seppuku of Yui Shōsetsu, the actual aftermath for his family).
- Gallery shows discovered endings as ink-stamped cards; undiscovered as blank
  stamps with a one-line rumor hint ("a ending for those who pay in flesh…").

## 9. Writing style guide

- **Voice:** second person, present tense, dry and concrete. Hemingway meets a
  period chronicle. No exclamation points in narration. Humor allowed via
  understatement and NPC dialogue only.
- **Length caps:** event body ≤ 180 words; outcome ≤ 120 words; epilogues ≤ 250.
- **Japanese terms:** gloss once at first use per run, always in codex. Kanji shown
  decoratively beside section headers, never required for comprehension.
- **Violence:** outcomes state wounds in body-count reality, never gore-porn.
  Executions referenced, not depicted.
- **Class & outcast groups:** eta/hinin characters written as individuals with
  trades and dignity; the game's lens indicts the *system* through mechanics
  (they are the only people who'll do certain work, and the work is necessary).
- **Women:** period restrictions are *depicted as the difficulty they were*, never
  played for titillation; Tale 5's writers' checklist includes the Bechdel pass on
  its shelter-network chain.
- **Every event must pass the codex test:** "what true thing does this teach?" If
  the answer is nothing, the event gets a codex card or gets cut.
